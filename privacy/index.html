Privacy policy

Last updated 10 October 2026

The OpenPaste app keeps your clipboard history on your computer and sends nothing anywhere. This website uses no cookies, no analytics and no tracking.

The app

What it stores

OpenPaste records what you copy: text and its formatting, links, colors, images and the paths of copied files. With each item it keeps the app it came from, that app's icon, and when you copied or pasted it. It also stores your pinboards and your settings.

All of it lives in a plain folder on your computer, which you can open from Settings:

SystemFolder
macOS~/Library/Application Support/OpenPaste/data
Windows%APPDATA%\OpenPaste\data
Linux~/.config/OpenPaste/data

By default, unpinned items are removed after 30 days or once you pass 2,000 items; you can change both in Settings. Clear history in Settings deletes your history, and deleting the folder removes everything.

What it never records

  • Copies that password managers mark as private, using each system's convention for this. This is on by default.
  • Anything copied in an app on your ignore list. 1Password, Bitwarden, KeePassXC and other password managers are on it by default.
  • Anything you copy while capturing is paused.

What it sends

Nothing. The app makes no network requests: no account, no analytics, no crash reports and no update checks. A link you open from a card opens in your browser.

Permissions

On macOS, OpenPaste asks for Accessibility access so it can press ⌘V in the app you were using. It uses it for nothing else. Without it, OpenPaste copies the item and you paste it yourself.

This website

The site has no cookies, local storage, analytics or ads. A few things load from other services, and each of them sees your IP address and browser details, as any web server does:

  • GitHub Pages hosts the site, and the installers download from GitHub. See GitHub's privacy statement.
  • GitHub's public API supplies the latest version and star count: your browser asks for them when the page loads.
  • Google Fonts serves the typefaces. See Google's privacy policy.

The shelf on the home page and the live demo only see what you copy or paste on the page itself. They keep it in the page's memory and forget it when you close the tab.

Changes and questions

If this policy changes, this page will show the new date, and every change is public in the site's history on GitHub. OpenPaste is made by Alexander Silver. For questions, open an issue on GitHub.
